The grid

Every square asks one question: are these two the same person? Tap once for ✕ (no), twice for ✓ (yes), three times to clear.

Inside each block, every row and every column ends up with exactly one ✓. That one rule does a lot of the work.

Every puzzle has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess. If you're stuck, there's always a move you can work out.

Step 1 · Read it straight off

Easy puzzles only need these.

Say what it says

Ella wore the red cap.

Tick Ella / Red. ✓ Ella = Red

Tom didn't order ham.

✕ Tom = Ham

A tick crosses out its row and column

Once Ella is red, nobody else is red and Ella isn't any other colour. (With auto-cross on, the app does this for you.)

Last one standing

If every square but one in a row or column is crossed, the last one is the ✓.

The quiet "no"s

Of Gorge and Ringo, one swims for the Penguins and the other for the Otters.

You don't know which way round yet, but you do know neither swims for the Dolphins or the Seals, and Gorge and Ringo are different people.

Amy, Ben, the customer who had cola and the one who ordered tuna were four different customers.

Every pair in that list is a ✕: Amy isn't the cola drinker, cola isn't tuna, and so on.

Step 2 · One clue plus your grid

Medium puzzles need these: read a clue against what you've already marked.

Number clues rule out the ends

Susan paid £2 more than the customer who had the coffee.

Three things straight away:

  • Susan didn't have the coffee (she paid more than that person).
  • Susan can't be on the lowest prices: there has to be room £2 below her.
  • The coffee drinker can't be on the highest prices: there has to be room £2 above.

With prices £4 to £8: ✕ Susan = £4, £5 ✕ Coffee = £7, £8

"Lower than" and "ahead of" clues work the same way: the lower one can't be last, the higher one can't be first.

Number clues and your grid

John had a lane number one higher than Paul.

If your grid already says Paul can only be in lane 2 or 3, then John can only be in lane 3 or 4.

Either / or

Cara bought either nachos or a hot dog.

Cross out every other snack for Cara. Later, once one of the two is ruled out, the other is her ✓.

Carry facts across

Once you know Ben ordered the ham, Ben and "the ham customer" are the same person. Anything you know about one is true of the other: if Ben didn't pay £4, the ham customer didn't either. Look across every block for these.

Step 3 · Two clues together

Hard and Fiendish puzzles. No single clue gives you anything new, but two together do. Fiendish needs this many times over.

The "what if?" test

Pick a square and ask: what if this were a ✓? Follow what the clues then force. If you hit a dead end (someone with nowhere left to go, or a clue that can't be true) the square must be a ✕. That isn't guessing: you only mark it once you've shown the other way is impossible.

Audrey paid £2 more than the customer who ordered egg mayo.
Kevin, Ben, the egg mayo customer and the smoothie customer were four different customers.

Your grid says Kevin paid £5, £6 or £7. Could Ben have paid £6?

  • Audrey isn't egg mayo (she paid more than them) and isn't the smoothie (your grid), so with the second clue all five customers are different people.
  • Audrey and egg mayo are £2 apart: £4 & £6, £5 & £7 or £6 & £8. If Ben has £6, only £5 & £7 is left.
  • Now £5, £6 and £7 are all taken, so Kevin has nowhere to go. Dead end.

✕ Ben = £6 And the same test rules out the smoothie customer at £6.

Boxing in

John had a lane number one higher than Paul.

Your grid says Ringo wears red and Gorge wears yellow. John and Paul sit side by side, so Ringo and Gorge get the other two lanes: 3 & 4, 1 & 4, or 1 & 2. If your grid also says Ringo isn't in lane 4 and Gorge isn't in lane 1, then Ringo is never in lane 2 and Gorge is never in lane 3.

✕ Lane 2 = Red ✕ Lane 3 = Yellow

Tips

  • Start "what if?" with the square that has the fewest options left: dead ends come quicker.
  • Number clues pair well with "four different people" clues, as above.
  • Cross off a clue once everything it says is on your grid, but keep its facts in mind.
  • Stuck? A hint shows where to look first, and its "Why?" plays out the "what if?" for you.
